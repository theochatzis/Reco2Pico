\section*{1. The ladder}
One jet collection per step, all built on the same simulated events and all
matched to the same generator jet. The difference of the response between two
consecutive steps isolates one effect.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{ladder.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{2. Matching by ancestry, not by $\Delta R$}
Every Geant4 particle descends from one generator primary, and every generator
particle sits in at most one gen jet. The energy a jet shares with a gen jet is
therefore exact, whatever the magnetic field and the decays did to the
directions. The same scores are used at every step.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{ancestry_matching.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{3. Which Geant4 particles are clustered}
The terminal particles of the stored decay tree, with an energy clause so that
a continuing particle that radiated a soft secondary is not removed. The same
rule defines the ideal particle-flow list; Geant4's boundary-crossing flag is
not used because particles born inside the calorimeter never carry it.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{terminal_rule.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{4. The calorimeter response from the sim hits}
The calorimeter sim hits carry the id of the particle that entered the
calorimeter, so the deposits are summed per particle without clustering;
jets of the deposits give the calorimeter-only response, and an ideal particle
flow built from momenta and deposits shows what tracking recovers.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{calo_deposits.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{5. The reconstructed jets on the same ladder}
PF candidates are attributed to simulated particles (track association for
charged ones, sim-hit shares of the cluster cells for the others); what is not
attributed is pile-up, noise or fakes. The reconstructed jets are then matched
by the same ancestry scores, and the signal-only response separates the
pile-up from the reconstruction effects without matching pile-up conditions.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{reco_attribution.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{6. How the pile-up is removed: attribution, not subtraction}
Every PF candidate is split into the part that can be traced to a simulated
particle of the hard-scatter event and a remainder. Charged candidates are
traced through their track; neutral ones cell by cell through the simulated
deposits, capped by the simulated energy of the cell. The remainder is
pile-up, noise or a fake. The signal part of a jet is the sum of the traced
parts of its constituents, so no pile-up matching between samples is needed.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pileup_attribution.pdf}\end{center}

\section*{7. The particle-flow block tables}
For the reconstruction studies the Pico also keeps the full PF block
structure: the elements, the geometric links PF found between them, and the
elements each candidate finally used.
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{pf_block.pdf}\end{center}
